Zdjeslav Bluxa - DRAFT

Zdjeslav Gostyvojev Bluxa (Glagolitic: ⰈⰌⰅⰔⰎⰀⰂ ⰃⰑⰔⰕⰬⰂⰑⰟⰂ ⰁⰎⰖⰢⰀ; 1861-1924) was an Upper Pannonian Slavic Revival activist, politician, writer, traveller, inventor, underwater photography pioneer, fringe scientist, occultist, cult leader, probably a spy, and the first Prime Minister of the independent Upper Pannonia. Early years

[EDIT] Born in 1861, Zdjeslav and his twin brother Zbyslav were the eldest sons of Gostyvoj Bluxa — a prominent Slavic Revival activist — and Magrita Bregovska, an illegitimate daughter of the Pannonian nobleman Lajosz Leonard Bregovi. As a child he was curious, smart, active, and creative, already showing a talent for deception.

[UNWRAP] At age 5, an accident on a boat - Zbyslav drowned, Zdjeslav survived. A tragedy and life-lasting trauma for him - loved his twin brother very much.

Zdjeslav attended a classical gymnasium alongside his father's Slavic Sunday school. In 1877 he enrolled in the Pannonian Pedagogical Institute, which Gostyvoj had co-founded that same year. That year also brought the deaths of his father and Dometius Bobov — lifelong rivals and collaborators who killed each other in their final duel — leaving Zdjeslav, at sixteen, the head of the family. He took the responsibility seriously and remained devoted to his siblings and their children throughout his life.

Two years later, in 1879, Zdjeslav became the informal leader of the Young Pannonians, a circle of Slavic Revival enthusiasts that had coalesced around the Pedagogical Institute. At its productive peak in the 1880s, the circle generated much of what became canonical in Pannonian art and literature, with members including novelists, composers, playwrights, and painters. The wider circle organized cultural events and supported its members' work; the inner circle formed a secret society centered on an invented cult of the Slavic goddess Mora, impersonated by Zdjeslav's sister Kvjetana.

Through the Slavic movement Bluxa befriended Adalfrid Attila, head of the House of Bregovi and his maternal uncle, an adventurous traveller who had explored the Malay Archipelago, Africa, and the Near East. Bregovi gave Bluxa access to the family library at Bregovo Castle, including the occult and alchemical manuscripts assembled by Gergej Gabriel — a former head of the house, a Freemason and Rosicrucian of notably mystical inclinations.

The reading fed directly into Bluxa's first publication 1. In 1882 he presented his pseudo-scientific Venetic theory inspired by theosophy, occult doctrines, pan-Slavic nationalism, and astronomy books by Camille Flammarion. The Veneds or Venets, a name for the proto-Slavs attested in ancient sources, were theorized to have been Slavic-speaking, proto-Glagolitic-writing superhumans from Venus. Bluxa inverted the conventional etymology: the goddess Venus was named after the planet, not the reverse — the planet's name being the Venets' own. These cosmic beings had "upbred" and enlightened the "subhuman" natives of Earth, built a great prehistoric civilization, and were ultimately destroyed by a flood — reflected in the legends of Atlantis and Vineta, the latter being, in Bluxa's reconstruction, the original form.

The theory's implications were immediately practical. In 1884, convinced that Venice had been a Venetic settlement, Bluxa attempted underwater photography in the Venetian lagoon. Italian authorities, suspecting him of being an Austrian agent spying on the Italian navy, arrested and expelled him. In 1885–1886 Bluxa joined Adalfrid Attila Bregovi's expedition to New Guinea. Landing on the Ventanat islands — small islets east of New Guinea — he declared them an Upper Pannonian possession on the grounds that a supposedly Venetic colony had once stood there.

[UNWRAP] Underwater photography again, with improved camera of his own invention. Poor quality images of something loosely resembling ruins. After return in 1887, organized expibition of his photos and findings of "Venetic artifacts" (all forgery).

[UNWRAP] In 1888, published "Immersion as spiritual experience" 2. Described his mystical visions during deep diving (possibly hypoxia-induced hallucinations). Speculated that "borderline deadly water element immersion" could bring contact with spiritual world, and this experience could be collective. Speculated further that ancient Venets could deliberately make their civilization be submerged, aiming to obtain supernatural powers; that before their Earth invasion, they might do the same on the entire Venus; that the contemporary Venetian "crypto-Slavic" ruling aristocracy may have deliberately doing the same with Venice, aiming to obtain world power. Some have believed; Komarovicki from "Young Pannonians" was inspired to write his "The Drowned World" symphony.
