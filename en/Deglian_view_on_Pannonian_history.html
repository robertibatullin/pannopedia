Deglian view on Pannonian history

This article summarizes the Deglian version of Former Upper Pannonia's (FUP) history, at the points where it diverges from the Zgurian version. This account appears in textbooks published in the Deglian Republic, underpins its official historical memory, and is shared by most Deglians apart from a small number of nonconformists.

Origins of the Pannonian peoples

Deglians are the indigenous population of Upper Pannonia, an authentically Slavic people related to the Dacians of ancient sources. They were the dominant nation in Great Moravia and the medieval Pannonian principalities, and the language of the medieval Glagolitic manuscripts is Deglian.

During the Ottoman period, Zgurians — literally "highlanders," a barbarian Balkan tribe of presumed Albanian origin — migrated into Upper Pannonia. They adopted the language and culture of the more advanced Slavic population, forgot their own origins, and began laying claim to the authentic Pannonian-Slavic — that is, Deglian — heritage, to which they had no right whatsoever. The name "Zgurian" does not appear in any source before the eighteenth century, evidence of how recently they arrived. True, the name "Deglian" is likewise absent, but that is simply because so few sources survive, and because medieval authors preferred the more prestigious classical term "Pannonians."

The Slavic Revival

In the 1840s, the great Deglian enlightener Enjo Zslutovodyj brought the Deglian Glagolitic tradition to scholarly attention and began publishing a journal in the Deglian language, though following tradition he called it Pannonian. In that same era, during the 1848 "Springtime of Nations," the Deglians awoke to political life and rallied behind the broader South Slavic cause, sending the volunteer "Deglian Hajduks" to fight in Ban Jelačić's army. The Zgurians, meanwhile, showed their true political colors by backing the Hungarian Revolution, a mortal enemy of the Slavs.

In the 1850s, the great Deglian scholar Dometius Bobov devised a Latin-alphabet system (Bobovica) far better suited to the Deglian language than Glagolitic. Zgurian scheming kept it from ever being adopted. Bobov also established, once and for all, the true historical origins of the Deglians and the Zgurians. His lifelong enemy and rival was the talentless charlatan Gustav Bloch, an ethnic German who threw in with the Zgurians purely out of spite and envy toward Bobov. Posing treacherously as Bobov's best friend, Bloch wormed his way into his confidence, inserted himself into every one of his projects to sabotage them, and repeatedly tried to have Bobov killed by provoking duels. He finally succeeded — though by accident, he was killed himself in that same final duel.

Besides Bobov, the greatest Deglian genius of the era was the poet Branko Dundulicz, whose heartfelt poems celebrated the beauty of the Deglian land and mourned the bitter fate of a Deglian nation oppressed by the Hungarians and deceived by the scheming Zgurians. The Zgurians themselves, through their circle of so-called Young Pannonians, did everything they could to persecute and ridicule Dundulicz, weaving the basest intrigues to seize the commanding heights of the emerging independence movement. They succeeded: the Pannonian National Union (PNU) ended up entirely dominated by a tightly-knit Zgurian clan.

The era of independence

The independent Republic of Upper Pannonia was proclaimed in 1918 at the instigation of the Zgurian clan, over the objections of the Deglian representatives in the PNU, who had argued for joining the new South Slavic state instead. That choice proved fatal: small, weak Pannonia, dominated as it was by Zgurians with their characteristic cosmopolitanism and indifference to their homeland's fate, fell easy prey to Hungarian aggression in 1940. Had Pannonia joined Yugoslavia from the start, as the Deglians wanted, who knows how history might have unfolded? Perhaps that addition would have strengthened Yugoslavia enough to hold out against the Germans in 1941.

Whatever the case, Zgurians effectively dominated the country through the First Republic, the First Kingdom, and the Communist period alike. Deglians were oppressed and denied their national rights: their language, alphabet, and every mark of national identity were banned. Official ideology — on this one point, all three regimes agreed — treated Deglians as merely part of a fictitious "Pannonian" nation, which in practice meant their assimilation by the Zgurians. Yet despite brutal repression, Deglian national consciousness never died, and underground movements for cultural autonomy persisted even through the harshest years of the dictatorships of Glovacz (Zgurian on his mother's side, incidentally) and Gowbka (a pure-blooded Zgurian).

Civil war and the current situation

After the fall of the Communist regime, Deglians could finally speak freely. At first they asked only for cultural and linguistic autonomy, and for Deglian interests to be taken into account during privatization. The Zgurian political elite answered these modest and just demands with an outburst of furious xenophobia, repression, and outright violence. Deglians had no choice but to defend themselves; the self-defense militias they began forming in 1991–1992 existed for that purpose alone, to protect against Zgurian pogromists. The Third Republic, still dominated by Zgurians, neither could nor wanted to shield Deglians from the violence, so they had no option but to rely on themselves and build their own state institutions.

Both sides, of course, committed violence during the 1992–1995 civil war, and Deglians were no angels either. But the Zgurians committed far more atrocities, and — this is the crucial difference — theirs was a deliberate policy of terror and genocide against the Deglians, whereas the isolated atrocities on the Deglian side were the excesses of individual field commanders.

The Western powers took an openly pro-Zgurian stance, backing Upper Pannonia's "territorial integrity" and condemning the Deglians as separatists. Western sanctions hit the Deglians hard while remaining purely symbolic against the Zgurians. The Lausanne Agreements, in effect, worked entirely in the Zgurians' favor: the UN Neutral Zone borders only Zgurian territory, and the Office of the High Commissioner maintains its closest ties precisely with the Zgurian authorities — most likely because the Zgurians have simply bought them outright. Small wonder, then, that the Hague Tribunal demands the extradition of virtually the entire Deglian military leadership, while settling for a handful of Zgurian small fry, whom the Zgurians sacrifice with characteristic treachery.

In 1999, liberal national traitors, having pulled the wool over the voters' eyes, came to power in the Deglian Republic, intending to hand over to The Hague everyone it demanded. This would have decapitated the republic and left it easy prey for the Zgurians. Fortunately, honest patriotic officers stepped in, arrested the traitors on entirely lawful grounds, and imposed martial law — a measure essential to the nation's survival. Ever since, the West and the UN administration have not stopped accusing the legitimate Deglian government of "dictatorship" and "totalitarianism," while turning a blind eye to the appalling corruption and flagrant human-rights abuses in the so-called "Zgurian Republic."

Deglians have long been ready for reconciliation. The Deglian position calls for mutual recognition of both republics as independent states along the existing line of division, on condition that the current Neutral Zone and a historically Deglian territory of Medlinska Plonina now held by the Zgurians be transferred to the Deglian Republic. Deglians are even willing to compromise, letting the Zgurians keep an other historically Deglian territory of Dobrogosczi Dubri. But the Zgurians will not hear of it, and their stubbornness is the chief reason for the deadlock in negotiations and the country's suspended status; it is the Zgurians, and only the Zgurians, who make it impossible to reach a final settlement and secure international recognition for both states — recognition that would finally give them a chance at normal development. Evidently, FUP's current status as a legal gray zone in the middle of Europe suits the criminal Zgurian leadership and their partners in the UN administration just fine.
