Ljubovan Jazvjec — DRAFT

Ljubovan Petrov Jazvjec (Zgurian; Deglian: Lübovan Petrov Jazvёc; Glagolitic: ⰎⰣⰁⰑⰂⰀⰐ ⰒⰅⰕⰓⰑⰂ ⰡⰈⰂⰟⰜ; 1935–1991) was an Upper Pannonian politician. He began his career as a Communist Party functionary and, after the collapse of socialism, quickly reinvented himself as a Zgurian nationalist and one of the leading political figures of the country's collapse and disintegration era. His untimely death spared him a role in the civil war — though it may also have helped bring the war closer.

Career in the People's Republic (1935–1989)

Jazvjec was born into a poor peasant family — the standard formula of official Communist biographies — and belonged to the Zgurian people. During the Hungarian occupation, his father was conscripted into the Hungarian Royal Army, fought on the Eastern Front, and went missing at Stalingrad: a fact official biographies passed over in silence. The rest of the biography followed the same well-worn pattern: village school, conscript service in the People's Liberation Army, work as a construction laborer in Vronovica, grassroots activism, membership in the Communist Party of Upper Pannonia (CPUP) from 1959, and entry-level party work in the committee of his construction enterprise.

In 1965, Jazvjec attended the CPUP's Twelfth Party Congress as a delegate from his local party organization, where he caught the eye of Supreme Leader Severyn Gowbka himself. His career took off from that moment. Malicious gossip attributed his rise to his appearance — short and prematurely bald — which Gowbka allegedly found endlessly amusing; according to this account, the generalissimo could never resist a grin at their meetings, or the chance to give Comrade Jazvjec's bald head a resounding slap.

Moreover, Ljubovan evidently had some genuine talent for administration, since he was entrusted with serious posts: chairman of the construction workers' trade union Party committee, head of the housing-construction section of the Vronovica city Party committee, chairman of the district committee of the Seventh District (during the peak of its large-scale development), and finally, in 1981, chairman of the Vronovica city Party committee itself. As party boss of the capital, he held one of the highest offices in the CPUP, with an accompanying seat on the Politburo.

As the capital's mayor, in effect, Jazvjec earned a reputation as a businesslike, pragmatic administrator who competently handled the mounting problems of the socialist economy's slow decline.

In 1988, however, he fell out of favor after criticizing Miljana Gowbka, the Supreme Leader's wife, for allegedly "pushing her fellow Deglians into every position." The remark was made in a small private circle, but it reached the generalissimo's ears quickly. Jazvjec was removed from his post "for health reasons" and expelled from the Politburo without delay; Gowbka's death on 10 November 1989 may well have saved him from worse consequences.
