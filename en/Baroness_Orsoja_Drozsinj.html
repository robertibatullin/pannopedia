Baroness Orsoja Drozsinj

Baroness Orsoja Drozsinj (Zgurian; Deglian: Orsoja Drožiń; Glagolitic: ⰑⰓⰔⰑⰡ ⰄⰓⰑⰆⰊⰐⰠ; Hungarian: Drozsinyi bárónő Orsolya; 1499-1536), born Orsola di Buonasperanza, was an Upper Pannonian noblewoman of the early 16th century. Her biography is little known and heavily mythologized.

Historical evidence

Orsola di Buonasperanza was born in a Dalmato-Italian patrician corsair family in Rijeka. In 1516, she was given in marriage to Upper Pannonian Baron Miklosz Drozsinj, owner of Drozsin castle in the Nexozsi Vrjexi mountains. In 1526, Baron Drozsinj was killed at the battle of Mohács. Orsoja inherited the castle as her husband's sole heir; the other male relatives had been killed or had fled to Royal Hungary, and there were no children.

After the battle of Mohács, the Ottomans seized most of the Hungarian Kingdom, including Upper Pannonia. They did not approach Drozsin castle, situated in the mountain wilderness. In the following years, according to a few surviving Austro-Croatian and Ottoman documents, the castle became a robbers' den. The robbers, dressed in wolf skins and calling themselves vlokodlaci (werewolves), attacked Christians and Muslims on both sides of the frontier.

After Kuzgun Qalesi was founded in 1534, Drozsin acquired strategic value, lying near the mountain pass on the road to the Adriatic Coast. The robbery could no longer be tolerated. In 1535, Abaz Bey Kuzgun, the first Ottoman Sancak Bey, besieged the castle. Orsoja refused to surrender. The siege lasted six months; all defenders died of starvation.

In legends

In local folklore, Orsoja is known as the "Werewolf Widow" and the "Cannibal Baroness": she is said to have been an actual werewolf who forced her captives to eat human flesh, thus turning them into werewolves as well. According to legend, she was the last survivor of the siege, having cannibalized all other castle residents. The ruins of the castle, destroyed by the Turks, were long considered cursed.

In literature

In 1886, Volimir Slezenj, a writer and Slavic Revival enthusiast from the Young Pannonians circle, published the historical novel "Orsoja Drozsinj", in which he depicted her as a heroic figure of national anti-Turkish resistance. Her "werewolf" robbers were presented as patriotic partisans, and the whole legendary tradition was dismissed as Turkish propaganda. The book proved influential; it was adapted into "Dawn over the Drava", the first Pannonian sound film, starring Etelka Baumgarten.
