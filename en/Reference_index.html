Here is an index of Pannonian names, toponyms and terms from other wiki pages.

.fup — internet top-level domain for Former Upper Pannonia, administered by UNOMFUP's Administrative Affairs Department; see UNOMFUP

Adelj — Queen consort of King Drogan I; see Queen Adele

Administrative Affairs Department (AAD) — UNOMFUP department controlling EU subsidies, business licensing, visas, and the FUP Clearing House; by common consent the most powerful department of the Mission; see UNOMFUP

Angelo De Coronatis — Italian former parliamentarian; UN High Commissioner for Former Upper Pannonia since 2018; see UNOMFUP

Balnosz — oil town in the northern uplands of Former Upper Pannonia; in Zgurian territory; in economic decline since oil extraction peaked

Banov polac — “Ban's Palace”; mid-19th century neo-Gothic building on the Fortress Platz (Grodski plac) in Vronovica; currently the official residence of the UN High Commissioner

baphotherapy — pseudo-medical “healing immersion” method combining mineral baths, mild induced hypoxia, and “spiritual healing”; developed by Zdjeslav Bluxa at Gnilovody; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Beczki most — “Viennese bridge”; northwestern bridge over the Vronovica island canal (Grodski ruv), connecting to the Vienna road

Beksziczi — former noble estate south of Vronovica, converted in 1950 into a labor camp where imprisoned writers produced novels under Dominik Raczev's direction; shut down by Gowbka in 1964; see Dominik Raczev

Benjamin Schwach — film director; ran Panoskop studio in the early 1930s; directed Zarja nad Dravow (1930) and The Muse (1931); brother of Raphael Schwach; became the Glovacz regime's chief court filmmaker; see Queen Adele

bluxovica — Zgurian Latin alphabet, invented by Dr. Gostyvoj Bluxa in 1855; see Pannonian language

bobovica — Deglian Latin alphabet, invented by Dr. Dometius Bobov in 1856; see Pannonian language

Boleslava Husarova (1922–1992) — secretary and later wife of Dominik Raczev; kept the family and Beksziczi archive while secretly informing on her husband to NON; see Dominik Raczev

Borko — nickname of Boris Gowbka, son of Severin Gowbka; see Severin Gowbka

Branko Dundulicz — reclusive Pannonian poet (c. 1830 – after 1887) whose true identity was never established; creator of the literary Pannonian language and its poetics; author of “Krasnej sulna” (1849), On the Ruins of Drozsin Castle (1854), The Captive Mouflon (1866), and Branko the Deglian (1883); see Branko Dundulicz

Bregovo — former Pannonian town; site of the medieval episcopal seat, pillaged and burned by Ottoman forces in 1529; later drowned after construction of the Gowbka dam; only the ruins of the bishop's castle survive, surrounded by water

Brotherhood of Peoples Tunnel — ceremonial name given to the Zorzsin Tunnel at its 1960 opening by Severin Gowbka and Josip Broz Tito; see Zorzsin Tunnel

Budajski most — “Buda bridge”; northeastern bridge over the Vronovica island canal, connecting to the Budapest road

Burza — “Stock Exchange”; 1890 building in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

Byla Gorna Pannonia — “Former Upper Pannonia”; the Zgurian and Deglian name for the territory; used in Pannonian-language documentation

Celestyn Olesczan — Count, pro-Hungarian Ban of Upper Pannonia; dissolved the Subor and launched a crackdown on the Slavic movement in 1896; see Krasnej sulna

City Management Department (CMD) — UNOMFUP department that governs the Neutral Zone and commands the Separate Police Division; see UNOMFUP

County of Upper Pannonia — northern and western portion of Upper Pannonia under Habsburg administration after 1691; merged with the Generalate of Upper Pannonia into the Banate of Upper Pannonia in 1850; see Former Upper Pannonia

Czelenica — oil town in the northern uplands of Former Upper Pannonia; in Zgurian territory; in economic decline since oil extraction peaked

czerny pomoczi — “black suspenders”; colloquial name for the zmocznjaki, the uniformed formations of the National Empowerment Movement; see King Drogan

Czigra — old town on the south bank of the South Drava, partially submerged when the Gowbka dam flooded the valley; its moss-covered ruins rise above the Xljubiscza marsh; see Xljubiscza

Czigraks — legendary former residents of drowned Czigra, said to have stayed in their homes after the flooding and turned into mutants or swamp spirits; see Xljubiscza

Czistovodno — “Clean Water”; abandoned Communist-era prison labor camp 4 km upstream of Gowbkaslava, site of a failed 1960s wetland “reclamation” project; see Xljubiscza

Czrevena Nicz — “Red Thread”, a Communist era name of a Vronovica textile factory, the successor to the nineteenth-century Sedelmeier Textile works

Darislav (Darko) Bregovi (1905–1951) — son of Otto Ostrovit Bregovi, younger brother of Svjetovit; anarchist; fought for Republican Spain; general and later Marshal in Gowbka's People's Liberation Army; see House of Bregovi

Decretum Ioannis — purported ninth-century papal grant cited by Klykov monastery as the basis for its perpetual Glagolitic autonomy; disputed by the bishops of Bregovo for centuries; established as an eleventh-century forgery by Zslutovodyj in 1847; see Klykov monastery, Enjo Zslutovodyj

degljanje — Slavicized ethnonym for the Deglian people, appearing in early 19th-century sources; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Degljanska Republyka — “Deglian Republic”; de facto state administering the south-eastern portion of Former Upper Pannonia; governed since 1999 by the Secret Control Committee under permanent martial law

Degljanska Socialystycka Partya (DSP) — Deglian Socialist Party, breakaway faction of the Socialist Party formed in the April 1990 split; ran a joint slate with the Deglian People's Front in the 1990 elections and soon merged into it; see Ljubovan Jazvjec

Degljanski narodny front (DNF) — Deglian People's Front, radical-nationalist Deglian party formed ahead of the 1990 elections, led by dissident Kosta Raczev; absorbed the Deglian Socialist Party after the elections; see Ljubovan Jazvjec

Didrik Darislav Bregovi (1750–after 1768) — son of Konrad Kazimir Bregovi, elder brother of Lipot Lotar; disappeared mysteriously in 1768, never found dead or alive; see House of Bregovi

Dietrich von Auffenstein — Count, Ban of Upper Pannonia from November 1898; lifted the ban on Pannonian newspapers and relegalized the PNU after Mourning Thursday; see Mourning Thursday

Divadlov trug — “Theatre Square”; square in the Second District of Vronovica, at the eastern end of Kolostanska ulica

Dobrogosczi Dubri — forested karst region in the western Nexozsi Vrjexi, near the Croatian and Slovenian borders; home to the glazsiczane; nationalized as a nature reserve in 1946; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

Dom djectva — “Childhood House”; orphanage in the Sixth District of Vronovica, built under the auspices of Miljana Gowbka; formerly housed over 2,000 miljaniczi; now the Military Academy of the Deglian Republic

Dominik Raczev (1909–1972) — Upper Pannonian writer and classic author of Upper Pannonian socialist realism; theorist and director of the Beksziczi writers' camp; see Dominik Raczev

Drava — the name of the PRUP's domestically manufactured microcar; see Drava

Drjezsavno divadlo — “National Theatre” (Royal Theatre under the Habsburgs); Italianate building of 1878 in Vronovica's Second District

Drjezsavny planovy komitet — “State Planning Committee”; PRUP central economic planning authority; its headquarters building on Victory Square (Trug Pobjedy) in the Sixth District, a Stalinist terraced skyscraper, has been occupied by the Secret Control Committee since 1999

Drogan Glovacz — fascist dictator of Upper Pannonia (1931–1934) and King Drogan I (1934–1940)

Drogomir Petrov — Cardinal of Vronovica; successor of Cardinal Lukas Rudecki; administered the diocese in exile from 1972; permitted to return in 1990; elevated to Cardinal in 1991; attempted mediation during the civil war; present at the assassination of Queen Ljudmila (1994); see Catholic Church in Upper Pannonia

Droguczin Mlynov — 18-year-old member of the Scorpions; assassinated Ban Celestyn Olesczan on Easter Sunday 1898; hanged 3 November 1898, precipitating Mourning Thursday; see Mourning Thursday

Drozsin — settlement on the approach to the Nexozsi Vrjexi passes; see Former Upper Pannonia

Dujek — stock comic character of Pannonian folk puppet theater, a wild simple-hearted highlander representing the Deglian stereotype; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Dujka — female counterpart of Dujek in Pannonian folk puppet theater; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Dviga o drjezsavnoj zmoczi — “National Empowerment Movement”, a Fascist-like political party led by Drogan Glovacz

Dviga o zabrone zgurcev — Movement for the Defense of Zgurians, a Zgurian political movement led by Ljubovan Jazvjec in 1991

echolithoscope — primitive sonar device invented by Zdjeslav Bluxa to locate underwater stone constructions; later adapted as a naval mine detector; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Enjo Zslutovodyj — founding father of the Pannonian Slavic Revival movement, early 19th century; avenue in Vronovica named after him; see Enjo Zslutovodyj

Etelkiny cicki — “Etelka's tits”; popular nickname for the low hemispherical domes of the Cathedral of SS. Cyril and Methodius in Vronovica, after the actress Etelka Baumgarten (stage name Adele Verger; later Queen Adele), noted for her slight figure

FUP Clearing House — Austrian-registered institution holding the only correspondent accounts at European banks available to the ZR and DR; required intermediary for all cross-border payments since 2003; see UNOMFUP

FUPcore — music genre of the sedmaki subculture; see Sedmaki

FUPFOR — NATO-led peacekeeping contingent deployed in Former Upper Pannonia since 1995; its Separate Police Division serves as the Neutral Zone's police force under UNOMFUP's City Management Department; see FUPFOR

Gabriel Klöckner — UN High Commissioner for Former Upper Pannonia, December 2017 to 2018; attempted to purge UNOMFUP's bureaucracy; forced to resign after harassment accusations; see UNOMFUP

Galeria sudjennoj umjetnosczi — “Modern Art Gallery”; Sezession building of 1910 in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

Generalate of Upper Pannonia — southern and eastern portion of Upper Pannonia after 1691, organized as part of the Military Frontier directly subordinate to military authorities; merged with the County of Upper Pannonia into the Banate of Upper Pannonia in 1850; see Former Upper Pannonia

Glazsica Valley — valley within Dobrogosczi Dubri containing the villages of Old Glazsica and New Glazsica, home to the glazsiczane; site of 1994–1995 civil war fighting; now under Deglian Republic control; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

glazsiczane — inhabitants of the Glazsica Valley, genetically isolated for millennia and traditionally shunned by neighboring communities for their familiarity with Dobrogosczi Dubri; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

glyphic discipline — esoteric teaching developed at Klykov monastery to justify Glagolitic adherence; held each Glagolitic character to represent an aspect of God, and their combinations to carry mystical meaning; prescribed prayers of character names recited in seemingly meaningless sequences; condemned by papal decree of 1509 as glypholatry; practiced at Klykov well into the nineteenth century; see Klykov monastery

glypholatry — “character worship”; name given by a papal decree of 1509 to the esoteric teaching of Klykov monks (the glyphic discipline); see Klykov monastery

Gnilovody — hot-spring resort in the northern uplands of Former Upper Pannonia; see Former Upper Pannonia

Goran Raxovecki — led the radical wing that broke from the Pannonian National Union in the 1890s to found the Scorpions (Sczury) underground organization; see Mourning Thursday

Gorny most — “Upper Bridge”; western bridge of the Bridged Ring (Mostovano kolo) over the South Drava, connecting the right bank to Slavjanski trug on the northern bank

Gowbczi skrynki — “Gowbka boxes”; four-story prefabricated concrete panel apartment blocks built in Vronovica in the 1950s

Gowbczi szatniki — “Gowbka wardrobes”; eight-story prefabricated apartment blocks built in Vronovica from the 1960s

Gowbkaslava — industrial city; transformed from a minor market town into the PRUP's primary manufacturing center

Gowbkaslavska jezda — “Gowbkaslava Avenue”; main north-west to south-east axis of the Seventh District in Vronovica; Communist-era name retained unchanged

Gowbkaslavska samojezdova djeljnja — “Gowbkaslava Automobile Plant”; manufacturer of the Drava car; see Drava

Graliscze Dynamo — Dynamo Stadium

Graliscze Krola Drogana — “Stadium of King Drogan”; national stadium in Vronovica, built 1937, capacity 30,000

Grod — colloquial euphemism for the Ministry of People's Security (NON), from its headquarters in the Vronovica island fortress

Grodski plac — “Fortress Platz” (also simply Plac); the central triangular junction inside the Vronovica island fortress, where the three gate-streets meet

Grodski ruv — “Fortress Canal”; the canal excavated in the 1710s to enclose the northern side of the Vronovica island fortress, separating it from the northern bank of the South Drava

gruszevica — pear brandy; the rakia preferred by Deglians; counterpart to Zgurian slivovica; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Helena Prox — distinguished Upper Pannonian writer; sent to the Mjedno camp after the 1950 assassination attempt against Gowbka, then transferred to the Beksziczi writers' camp; released in 1960 after serving most of her ten-year sentence; see Dominik Raczev

International Criminal Tribunal for Former Upper Pannonia — war crimes tribunal established following the 1992–1995 civil war; issued indictments against Deglian and Zgurian commanders; Martyn Bregovi among those indicted; see House of Bregovi

Isztvan Koczar — Pannonian architect and painter (1865–1940); member of the Young Pannonians circle; monumental canvases Cyril and Methodius Enter Moravia (1890) and The Fall of Bregovo (1897); built the Cathedral of SS. Cyril and Methodius (Vronovica, consecrated 1932)

Jan Hofmann — architect of the Sedelmeier Passage (Sedelmajerov passazs); see Second District of Vronovica

Jarka — first wife of Ljubovan Jazvjec; filed for divorce in 1988 after his fall from favor; see Ljubovan Jazvjec

Jezda Druzsby narodov — “People's Friendship avenue”; Communist-era name for the south-western radial avenue of Vronovica's Fifth District; notable as the site of some of the civil war's worst atrocities; retained on Zgurian-zone maps

Jezda Enja Zslutovodyj — “Enjo Zslutovodyj avenue”; principal avenue of Vronovica's Second District and Neutral Zone, running north-east from the Bridged Ring; formerly King Franz Joseph avenue (Ferenc József király út)

Jezda Jednosczi — “Unity Avenue”; main south-western axis of the Seventh District in Vronovica, running north-east to south-west as the direct continuation of People's Friendship Avenue from the Fifth District; Communist-era name retained unchanged

Jezda Kommunizma — “Communism avenue”; central axis of the Sixth District in Vronovica, renamed Jezda Slobody (Liberty avenue) after 1990

Jezda Narodnoj oslododjej raczi — “People's Liberation Army avenue”; main north-south axis of Sockostur, renamed Jezda Oslododji after 1990

Jezda Nepodstoja — “Independence avenue”; the main avenue running south from the Turkish bridge across the South Drava in Vronovica, continuing as Jezda Oslododji through the Deglian zone

Jezda Oslododji — “Liberation avenue”; the post-1990 name for Jezda Narodnoj oslododjej raczi, the main north-south axis of Sockostur

Jezda Slobody — “Liberty avenue”; post-1990 name for Jezda Kommunizma (Communism avenue), the central axis of the Sixth District in Vronovica

Jezda Socialyzma — “Socialism avenue”; Communist-era name for the south-eastern radial avenue of Vronovica's Fifth District; retained on Zgurian-zone maps

Josias Grottner — kapellmeister of the Ban's court orchestra; set “Krasnej sulna” to music in 1854; see Krasnej sulna

Juzsna Drava — the South Drava river, the primary Pannonian river and the boundary dividing Vronovica

Juzsny kolostan — “South Station”; principal railway terminus for the southern and eastern approaches to Vronovica, in the Fifth District; late Habsburg structure comprehensively rebuilt under Gowbka in the 1950s

Kapitul — “The Chapter”; late-18th century Classicist building on the Fortress Platz (Grodski plac) in Vronovica; houses the Catholic diocesan administration

karakondjul — faun-like hybrid demon of pan-Balkan folklore, said to inhabit the caves of Dobrogosczi Dubri; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

Klyci — “Fangs”; the three sandstone pillar rocks above the South Drava valley on which Klykov monastery is built; see Klykov monastery

Klykov — mountain location above the South Drava valley; site of a monastery that maintained the Slavic Glagolitic liturgical tradition from the early medieval period; designated a cultural heritage monument under the Communist government; see Klykov monastery

knjuz biskup — “Prince Bishop”; secular-ecclesiastical title granted by the Hungarian crown to the Bregovo bishops in 1115, conferring limited secular authority over Upper Pannonia; see Bregovo

Kolostan Strangler — unidentified serial killer who garroted up to seventeen passersby near the North Station between 1893 and 1896; widely believed to have been Ljudomir Bregovi; see House of Bregovi, Fourth District of Vronovica

Komarovica — settlement on the South Drava upstream of Vronovica; see Former Upper Pannonia

Kosturska naprava — “City Administration”; the Habsburg municipal building on Jezda Enja Zslutovodyj in Vronovica, now housing the UN City Management Office

Kostursko divadlo — “Municipal Theatre”; theatre in Vronovica's Third District, historically operating in all three city languages; managed in the early 20th century by Leonard Baumgarten, father of Queen Adele; currently under Zgurian Republic cultural management

Krasimir Bluxa — brother of Zdjeslav Bluxa; medically trained; assisted with baphotherapy clinics in Gnilovody and Paris; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Krasnej sulna — “Fairer than the Sun”; former state anthem of Upper Pannonia, written 1849 by Branko Dundulicz; see Krasnej sulna

Krolevy Zseljezni — “King's Iron”; settlement at the foothills of the Nexozsi Vrjexi; site of iron ore deposits; in Deglian territory

kto xody po rextu — “those who go by rules”; Zgurian sedmak self-designation, rejecting the term sedmak as a Deglian insult; see Sedmaki

Kvjetana Bluxa — daughter of Gostyvoj Bluxa (1862–1938); muse of the Young Pannonians circle; married Zlotan Czigraj in 1884

Lausanne Agreements — 1995 agreement between the parties to the 1992–1995 civil war establishing UNOMFUP, the Neutral Zone, and the division of Former Upper Pannonia into the Deglian and Zgurian Republics; see UNOMFUP

ljelik — shapeshifting bat of folklore said to haunt Dobrogosczi Dubri, appearing in nightmares and dragging people from the waking world into the world of dreams; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

Ljubovan Jazvjec — Zgurian politician; former head of the Vronovica city committee of the Communist Party; leader of the Movement for the Defense of Zgurians; died 28 August 1991 in a fall triggered by the earthquake following the Zorzsin Tunnel's collapse; see Zorzsin Tunnel

Ljudmila — daughter of Queen Adele; proclaimed Queen of Upper Pannonia in exile (1952); assassinated during her enthronement ceremony (1994); see Queen Adele

Ljudomir Bregovi (1875–1896) — younger brother of Otto Ostrovit Bregovi; widely believed to have been the Kolostan Strangler; see House of Bregovi

Lubovincza Gora — “Skull Mountain” (from lubovina, “skull”); the steep, pinewood-covered slope from which the three Klyci pillar rocks rise; site of Klykov monastery

Lukas Rudecki — Bishop (later Cardinal) of Vronovica; elevated to Cardinal; participated in the enthronements of King Drogan I and Queen Adele; defended Jews and Roma during the German occupation; forced to emigrate to Italy; canonized; see Catholic Church in Upper Pannonia

madjaron (pl. madjarony) — “Hungaricizer”; contemptuous term for a Pannonian who adopted a pro-Hungarian stance or identity

Magdolna — femme fatale character played by Adele Verger in the 1931 film The Muse, modeled on Kvjetana Bluxa; see Queen Adele

Martyn the Prince (born 1965) — younger brother of Mixal Bregovi; placed in the Childhood House in 1979; Deglian field commander of the “Vipers” formation in the civil war, styling himself “Prince Bregovi”; indicted for war crimes; no information available since 1999; speculated to be a member of the Secret Control Committee; see House of Bregovi

Miljana Gowbka (born 1912) — née Skorczinska, wife of Severin Gowbka and perennial Chairwoman of the Legislative Assembly; see Severin Gowbka, House of Bregovi

miljaniczi — “Miljana's children”; orphans raised at the Childhood House (Dom djectva) under Gowbka's demographic policy

minik — private minibus (colloquial; pl. miniki)

Ministeria o finance — “Ministry of Finance” (pre-lexical-purification form; replaced by Naprava o skarbe under the First Republic)

Mjedno — site of the Communist-era political internment camp, partly located underground in the Bronze age copper mines

Mlodoborska liga — “Youth Fighter League”; underground youth wing of the Communist Party of Upper Pannonia

Mlodopannonci — “Young Pannonians”; informal circle of Slavic Revival enthusiasts active in Vronovica in the late 19th century; see Young Pannonians

Model 1971 rifle — standard small arm of the People's Liberation Army; an unlicensed copy of the Chinese Type 56, itself a copy of the Soviet AK; produced at a numbered plant in Gowbkaslava; see People's Liberation Army

Monitoring Department (MD) — UNOMFUP department that posts observer-commissioners to every ZR and DR government agency and keeps them under covert surveillance; see UNOMFUP

Mora — Slavic goddess at the center of a secret cult invented by Zdjeslav Bluxa within the Young Pannonians circle, impersonated by his sister Kvjetana Bluxa; see Zdjeslav Bluxa, Young Pannonians

Moritz Sedelmeier — arts patron and textile merchant; funded the Sedelmeier Passage and the Modern Art Gallery in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

Mostovano kolo — “Bridged Ring”; the road circuit encircling the Vronovica island fortress, built 1870–1874; see Vronovica

Mourning Thursday — mass mourning-dress demonstration in Vronovica on 3 November 1898, the day of Droguczin Mlynov's execution; see Mourning Thursday

Muzeum historii i umjetnosczi — “History and Fine Arts Museum”; 1886 building in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

Naprava o skarbe — “Ministry of Finance” (post-purification Pannonian coinage, replacing the loan-based Ministeria o finance)

Naprava o zabrone naroda — “Ministry of People's Defence”, defence ministry of the People's Republic; territorial defence reported to it directly, bypassing district command; see People's Liberation Army

Naprava ob oxrone naroda (NON) — “Ministry of People's Security”; the Communist regime's secret police; see Severin Gowbka

Napred — do slavy! — “Forward — to glory!”; motto of the National Empowerment Movement (Dviga o drjezsavnoj zmoczi); see King Drogan

Narodna oslododja racz (NOR) — “People's Liberation Army”; armed forces of Upper Pannonia throughout the Socialist era; raised in 1942 as the armed wing of the UP National Liberation Committee, rebuilt after 1968 around territorial militia and total partisan war, and split along ethnic lines in 1991; see People's Liberation Army

Narodny polac — “People's Palace”; enormous unfinished government complex in Sockostur, construction begun 1971, never completed

Narodny zbor — “People's Club”; Communist-era repurposing of the Stock Exchange building (Burza) in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

Nastava pannonskoj mlodji — “Exhortation for the Pannonian Youth”; 1926 pamphlet by Drogan Glovacz

National Committee of Upper Pannonia — body formed in summer 1918 by Pannonian National Union figures; gained legal authority under Emperor Karl's October 1918 manifesto; declared Upper Pannonia's independence and formed the First Republic's Provisional Government; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Nejtralka — “the Neutral Zone”; colloquial Pannonian name for the UN-administered Neutral Zone of Vronovica

Nexozsi Vrjexi — “Unpassable Mountains” (lit. “No-Go Mountains”); the northernmost chain of the Dinaric Alps, forming the southern frontier of Former Upper Pannonia with Slovenia, Croatia and Serbia

Novokopy — coal-mining town in the northern uplands of Former Upper Pannonia; in Zgurian territory

Novoslava — “New Glory”; official name for Gowbkaslava since 1990; in practice unused

Novy universitet — “New University”; founded 1876 as Pannonian Pedagogical Institute by liberal pan-Slavicist professors in Vronovica; intellectual centre of Pannonian nationalism and alma mater of the First Republic's leadership; university status granted by the First Republic; primary national institution under the First Republic and the PRUP; sole functioning university of the Zgurian Republic since 1992; in the Third District

numbered plants — secret military factories in Gowbkaslava designated by arbitrary and frequently changed numbers rather than names; see Gowbkaslava, People's Liberation Army

Orsoja Drozsinj — noblewoman; born Orsola di Buonasperanza in Rijeka; owner of Drozsin castle after the battle of Mohács (1526); last defender of Drozsin against Ottoman siege (1535); subject of local folklore as the “Werewolf Widow” and “Cannibal Baroness”; see Baroness Orsoja Drozsinj

Pan Skuka — “Mr. Boredom”; nickname for Zlotan Czigraj among his peers in the Young Pannonians circle

Pannonia i jeju vrozi — “Pannonia and Its Enemies”; political book by Drogan Glovacz (1920); named Hungary, the SCS Kingdom, and domestic leftists and minorities as the three enemies of Pannonia; advocated “popular monarchy”; see King Drogan

Pannonian Committee in Petrograd — Russian-financed émigré political organization led by Zdjeslav Bluxa from 1914; recruited Pannonian POWs into what became the Pannonian Legion; see Zdjeslav Bluxa, Pannonian Legion

Pannonian Democratic Renovation Party (PDRP) — liberal party led into the 1990 elections by Ljubodar Vloszek, taking 7% of the vote and becoming arithmetically indispensable to any coalition; see Ljubodar Vloszek, Ljubovan Jazvjec

Pannonian National Union (PNU) — first legal Pannonian nationalist party, led by Zlotan Czigraj; won Subor elections for the first time in 1894; see Krasnej sulna

Pannonska drjezsavna racz (PNA) — “Pannonian National Army”; the Upper Pannonian state's armed force; see Pannonian Legion

Pannonsko Morje — “Pannonian Sea”, the reservoir created by the South Drava dam in 1954; originally the Stalin Reservoir; renamed in 1957; among the most polluted water bodies in Eastern Europe; see Gowbkaslava

Panoskop — Upper Pannonia's sole film studio in the early sound era; produced Zarja nad Dravow (1930) and The Muse (1931); see Queen Adele

partisanization — Pannonian term for the reorganization of the People's Liberation Army toward a territorial militia, begun in 1956 under Yugoslav influence; see People's Liberation Army

Peace Settlement Department (PSD) — UNOMFUP department running the perpetually stalled Zgurian–Deglian peace negotiations; see UNOMFUP

Peter Zorza — Baron (1828–1887), second son and later head of House Zorza; a leading candidate in theories identifying the true identity of the poet Branko Dundulicz; see Branko Dundulicz

Prjeva zroba — “First Product”; designation of the P-1 prototype Drava automobile; see Drava

Pucza — small market town on the South Drava; precursor settlement to Gowbkaslava; expropriated and demolished in 1950

Quadrilateral Commission — Vienna-based sixteen-member body (four each from the ZR, DR, EU, and NATO) that appoints and oversees UNOMFUP's High Commissioner; see UNOMFUP

Raphael Schwach — Third International delegate and leader of the 1919 Pannonian Soviet Republic; shot by Legion tribunals in 1919; brother of film director Benjamin Schwach; see Pannonian Legion, Queen Adele

rext — code of conduct of the sedmaki subculture (from German Recht, “right”); the binary system classifying all persons, objects, and behaviors as either vajs or szvarc; see Sedmaki

rextovy — “rule-abiding”; Zgurian self-designation for members of the sedmak subculture; see Sedmaki

Robocza jezda — “Worker's avenue”; arc-shaped avenue in Vronovica's Fifth District connecting the three radial avenues south of the Bridged Ring, marking the approximate boundary with Sockostur

Rudec — settlement downstream of Gowbkaslava near the Romanian border; see Former Upper Pannonia

Ruska ulica — “Russian Street”; street bounding the New University quarter in Vronovica's Third District

Sandor Ljux — Pannonian playwright, director, and actor (1862–1927); member of the Young Pannonians circle; author of A Hungarian Wedding (1885, banned), Peasants Are Humans Too (1889), and Something Is Wrong With This World (1895)

Sczury — “Scorpions”; underground terrorist organization founded by the radical wing of the Pannonian National Union after Ban Olesczan's 1895 crackdown; see Mourning Thursday

Second Easter — canonical nickname for Droguczin Mlynov's assassination of Ban Celestyn Olesczan on Easter Sunday 1898; see Mourning Thursday

Sedelmajerov passazs — “Sedelmeier Passage”; arcade completed 1903 in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

sedmajka — “Sevener” (fem.); female member of the sedmaki subculture; see Sedmaki

sedmak — “Sevener” (masc.); member of the sedmaki lower-class urban youth subculture; see Sedmaki

sedmaki — “Seveners”, plural of sedmak

Separate Police Division — police force of the Neutral Zone; nominally part of FUPFOR but placed under the command of UNOMFUP's City Management Department; see UNOMFUP

Severin Gowbka — Communist dictator of the People's Republic of Upper Pannonia, 1945–1989; see Severin Gowbka

sja oszvarcuje — reflexive verb; “defiles oneself” (through contact with something szvarc); implies defilement as an internal change of state; see Sedmaki

Sjeverny kolostan — “North Station”; principal passenger railway terminus of Vronovica, in the Fourth District, completed 1885; modelled on the Budapest Nyugati station; serves lines to Zgurian-controlled territory

Slavjanska jezda — “Slavic avenue”; main ceremonial avenue in Vronovica, renamed variously throughout the 20th century

Slavjanska rozbuda — “Slavic Awakening”; first Pannonian-language periodical, launched by Enjo Zslutovodyj in 1848 as a monthly, converted to a weekly in 1849; banned by Habsburg authorities the same year

Slavjanski trug — “Slavic Square”; circular junction at the north-western end of the Bridged Ring where Slavjanska jezda meets the Ring

Slavjansko divadlo — “Slavic Theatre”; theatre inaugurated 1876, designed by Władysław Gorczyński in a Russian neo-Byzantine idiom; principal cultural institution of the Zgurian Republic, performing exclusively in Zgurian since 1990

slivovica — plum brandy; the rakia preferred by Zgurians; counterpart to Deglian gruszevica; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Smrudno Morje — “Stinky Sea”; colloquial name for the Pannonian Sea (Pannonsko Morje), the reservoir created by the South Drava dam; see Gowbkaslava

smurfy — Zgurian slur for Deglians, adapted from the Smurfs franchise; one of several hundred Zgurian insults for Deglians, drawing on the blue-and-white color association; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Socialystycka Partya su Jednenow Pannoniju (SPJP) — Socialist Party for a United Pannonia, dwindling internationalist rump of the former Communist Party formed in the April 1990 split; failed to enter parliament in the 1990 elections; see Ljubovan Jazvjec

Sockostur — “Socialist City”; large Brutalist planned district in Vronovica's southern zone, built 1965 onwards; see Vronovica

Sokol — the pan-Slavic gymnastic and sports organization; as in the Sokol Sports Complex in the Third District

Spodny most — “Lower Bridge”; eastern bridge of the Bridged Ring (Mostovano kolo) over the South Drava, connecting to the right bank at the start of the Ring's southern arc

spremnica — female member of the spremniks subculture (fem. sg.); see Spremniki

spremnik — member of the spremniks militarized nationalist youth subculture (masc. sg.; lit. “one who is ready/prepared”); see Spremniki

spremniki — “those who are ready” or “those prepared” (for warfare); a militarized nationalist youth subculture; see Spremniki

St. Geonus the Stylite — Byzantine hermit monk; said to have been the first to settle on the Holy Spirit's rock at Klykov in the sixth century; founding figure of what became Klykov monastery; see Klykov monastery

St. Nectarius — abbot of Klykov monastery from 1310 until his death in 1339 (born 1267); renowned as a manuscript collector; local legend remembered him as a magician capable of commanding demons; see Klykov monastery

St. Tibor of Bregovo — first Bishop of Upper Pannonia (from 1040; seat at Bregovo); established the Pannonian diocesan seat and imposed the Latin rite on the Pannonian Church; canonized in the medieval period; the 1755 Vronovica cathedral was dedicated to him; see Catholic Church in Upper Pannonia

Stalinkostur — “Stalin City”; name given to Pucza upon its designation as the PRUP's primary industrial site in 1950; renamed Gowbkaslava in 1956; see Gowbkaslava

Starokopy — “Old Mines”; former coal-mining settlement in the northern uplands of Former Upper Pannonia; mines abandoned in the nineteenth century; effectively a ghost town; in Zgurian territory

Stary universitet — “Old University” (formally the Upper Pannonian University); founded as a Jesuite college in 1725; reorganized into Academy of Sciences by Maria Theresa in 1774; university status granted by Franz Joseph in 1876; taught exclusively in Latin until 1867; subordinated to the New University under the First Republic; closed 1991; reopened 1996 under the UNOMFUP with mandatory mixed Zguro-Deglian classes; Second District of Vronovica

Strjexovana truzsnica — “Indoor/Covered Market”; two-wing iron-and-brick market hall in Vronovica's Fourth District, opened 1888

Subor — Upper Pannonian Diet; the legislative assembly of Upper Pannonia under Habsburg rule; see House of Bregovi

Sunovrotov dom — “Narcissus House”; Art Nouveau building of 1907, Fourth District of Vronovica; originally a Gentlemen's Club (casino, cabaret, and bordello); closed by the Communists in 1945, its erotic interior decorations confiscated to Zeleny dvor; assigned to the Modern History Institute until 1990; independent art commune since 1997

Svetoplok — Pannonian given name; as in King Svetoplok International Airport, the international airport north of Vronovica, administered by FUPFOR

Svjetana Jozsefova — journalist, second wife of Ljubovan Jazvjec from September 1990; ran the Movement for the Defense of Zgurians' party media from 1991; see Ljubovan Jazvjec

Szamenik — coal-mining town in the northern uplands of Former Upper Pannonia; in Zgurian territory

szvarc — “black” (from German schwarz); the “defiling” category in the rext code of the sedmaki; applied to the enemy ethnic community, non-European races, and everything else classified as contaminating; see Sedmaki

szvulj — “gay” (from German slang schwul); category in the rext code covering homosexuality, LGBTQ+ identity, and all behaviors or appearances perceived as soft, cultivated, unmanly, nonviolent, tolerant, or intellectual; see Sedmaki

The ground must burn under the occupier's feet — slogan of the People's Liberation Army's post-1967 doctrine, invoked to justify the mass production of mines and mortars; see People's Liberation Army

The Muse — scandalous 1931 Panoskop film directed by Benjamin Schwach, starring Adele Verger as Magdolna; a thinly disguised dramatization of the Volimir Slezenj–Kvjetana Bluxa affair, widely read as a political attack on President Zlotan Czigraj; see Queen Adele

tovariscz — comrade (genderless form of address mandated under the Communist government 1945–1990; replaced by pan/pani after democratisation in 1990); see Pannonian language

Trug Baxa, Szvaxa i Karaczoni — “Bach, Schwach and Karácsony Square”; Communist-era name for Legion Square (Trug Legii) in Vronovica's Third District, after three leaders of the 1919 Bolshevik uprising

Trug Djectva — “Childhood Square”; square in Vronovica's Sixth District at the crossing of Ulica Pannonskix zsen with Liberty Avenue

Trug Enja Zslutovodyj — “Enjo Zslutovodyj Square”; circular junction at the north-eastern end of the Bridged Ring where Jezda Enja Zslutovodyj meets the Ring; contains the 1926 statue of Enjo Zslutovodyj

Trug Legii — “Legion Square”; central square in Vronovica's Third District, site of the Legislative Assembly and other governmental buildings; see Pannonian Legion

Trug Pobjedy — “Victory Square”; large ceremonial square in the Sixth District of Vronovica, on Liberation Avenue; site of the equestrian statue of Gowbka and the State Planning Committee building

Trug Promysla — “Industry Square”; central junction of the Seventh District in Vronovica, where Jezda Jednosczi crosses Gowbkaslavska jezda

Turski most — “Turkish bridge”; the Ottoman-era stone bridge over the South Drava in Vronovica, the city's oldest surviving structure

Ukrainska ulica — “Ukraine Street”; street bounding the New University quarter in Vronovica's Third District

Ulica Pannonskix zsen — “Pannonian Women's Street” (renamed Deglian Women's Street after 1995); runs north-west from the People's Palace to Childhood Square in Vronovica's Sixth District; lined with statues of archetypal socialist women

Universal Store No. 1 — Communist-era name for the Sedelmeier Passage in Vronovica's Second District; see Second District of Vronovica

Universitecka ulica — “University Street”; street in Vronovica's Second District on which the Old University stands

Universitecka zagroda — “University Park”; park forming one boundary of the New University quarter in Vronovica's Third District

UNOMFUP — United Nations Observer Mission for Former Upper Pannonia; international organization established 1995 under the Lausanne Agreements to monitor the ceasefire, pursue a peace settlement, and administer the Neutral Zone; see UNOMFUP

UP National Liberation Committee — Soviet-sponsored body constituted in 1942 under the nominal chairmanship of Severin Gowbka, with four NKVD officers attached; parent organization of the NOR; see Severin Gowbka, People's Liberation Army

Ursula Raczeva (born 1948) — daughter of Dominik Raczev; married and divorced novelist Ljubodar Vloszek (1969–1971); see Dominik Raczev, Ljubodar Vloszek

vajs — “white” (from German weiß); the “clean” category in the rext code of the sedmaki; applied to permissible persons, objects, and behaviors; see Sedmaki

Velikomoravski trug — “Great Moravian Square”; main square before the Cathedral of SS. Cyril and Methodius in Vronovica

Venetic theory — pseudo-scientific doctrine devised by Zdjeslav Bluxa in 1882, holding the Veneds/Venets (proto-Slavs) to have been Slavic-speaking superhumans from Venus; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Viktor Kostanj — Nobel Prize-nominated Upper Pannonian writer; sent to the Mjedno camp after the 1950 assassination attempt against Gowbka, then transferred to the Beksziczi writers' camp; went on strike in 1963 and was transferred to the Czistovodno labor camp, where he died the same year; see Dominik Raczev

Vilmosz Komarovicki — Pannonian composer and pianist (1861–1909); member of the Young Pannonians circle; author of the first Pannonian-language operas, including Prince Maczasz (1884), Zgurian Dances (1888), and The Witch Peak (1892)

Vipers — (1) colloquial nickname for the House of Bregovi, derived from the family coat of arms (coiled black viper on green background); (2) Deglian paramilitary formation in the 1992–1995 civil war, commanded by Martyn Bregovi (“Prince Bregovi”); see House of Bregovi

Visztica — settlement on the South Drava upstream of Vronovica; see Former Upper Pannonia

Vjedjmina Gora — “Witch Peak” (1,847 m); highest point of the Nexozsi Vrjexi range

vlokodlaci — “werewolf” (pl.); name adopted by the robber band operating from Drozsin castle in the 1520s–1530s; also the folkloric identity attributed to Baroness Orsoja Drozsinj; see Baroness Orsoja Drozsinj

Volimir Slezenj — Pannonian novelist and Slavic Revival enthusiast (1860–1892); member of the Young Pannonians circle; author of Burnt Alive (1884), Orsoja Drozsinj (1886), A Slavic Litany (1888), and the posthumous The Thorny Way of an Adolescent Pannonian (1895); see Baroness Orsoja Drozsinj, Young Pannonians

Vronov grod — “Raven's Castle”; the Ottoman-era island fortress in Vronovica, later the headquarters of NON

Vronovica — capital of Former Upper Pannonia; see Vronovica

Vronovica Military Academy — officer training institution of the People's Liberation Army, founded in the first years of the People's Republic; see People's Liberation Army

Xljubiscza — poisoned marshland region of Former Upper Pannonia along the South Drava, formed after the Gowbka dam flooded the valley in 1950–1954; see Xljubiscza

xljubovaty — “to xljub”; underworld punishment of stranding a person, usually naked, in Xljubiscza by boat at night; practiced since the socialist era; see Xljubiscza

Xodnica vitezej — “Alley of Heroes”; formal promenade in the Sixth District of Vronovica, extending 1 km east from Victory Square to the People's Palace grounds, flanked by sixteen granite statues of archetypal socialist figures

Zadravska — district of Vronovica south of the South Drava river; historically workers' housing, comprehensively rebuilt under socialism

Zadruga rozbudy — “Awakening Society”; Slavophile cultural society founded by Enjo Zslutovodyj in 1848; dissolved by Habsburg authorities in 1849; refounded 1852; see Enjo Zslutovodyj

Zagroda kuljturna pocziva — “Park of Cultured Leisure”; riverside park in the north-eastern part of Sockostur, modelled on the Moscow Gorky Park

Zakonodarno Zgromadjenje — “Legislative Assembly”; the Upper Pannonian parliament building in Vronovica, completed 1935 in stripped Rationalist style

Zarja nad Dravow — “Dawn Over the Drava”; first Upper Pannonian sound film (1930), starring Etelka Baumgarten (later Queen Adele)

Zbyslav Bluxa — twin brother of Zdjeslav Bluxa; drowned in a boating accident at age five; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Zdjeslav Bluxa — Pannonian nationalist politician; elder son of Gostyvoj Bluxa; see Zdjeslav Bluxa

Zeleny dvor — “Green Court”; Gowbka's private estate outside Vronovica, where he died in 1989

zgurci — Slavicized ethnonym for the Zgurian people, appearing in early 19th-century sources; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Zgurska Republyka — “Zgurian Republic”; de facto state administering the north-western portion of Former Upper Pannonia, comprising approximately 55 percent of the former country's area

Zlotan Czigraj — politician (1860–1936); leader of the Pannonian National Union, the first legal Pannonian nationalist party; first President of the First Republic (1920–1934); member of the Young Pannonians circle; known among peers as Pan Skuka (“Mr. Boredom”)

zmijevila — serpent-woman of folklore said to lure travelers into the ponor shafts of Dobrogosczi Dubri with her song; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

zmocznjaki — “empowerers”; uniformed political formations of King Drogan's National Empowerment Guard, popularly known as the “black suspenders”

Zorza — baronial family; see Dobrogosczi Dubri

Zorzsin — ancestral castle of House Zorza in the Nexozsi Vrjexi; the Zorzsin Tunnel, the range's single rail crossing until its 1991 destruction, is named after it; see Former Upper Pannonia, Branko Dundulicz

Zorzsin Tunnel — 12-km railway tunnel under the Nexozsi Vrjexi linking Former Upper Pannonia with Yugoslavia; built 1957–1960; destroyed by Serb saboteurs on 28 August 1991; see Zorzsin Tunnel

Zorzsinski Dubri — mountain resort near the Nexozsi Vrjexi; site of Ljubovan Jazvjec's fatal fall during the earthquake triggered by the Zorzsin Tunnel's 1991 collapse; see Zorzsin Tunnel

Zseljo “Spiderman” Totov — Deglian civil war commander, national hero in the Deglian zone and subject of outstanding international arrest warrants

Zsluty Vody — “yellow waters”; settlement in Upper Pannonia, named for its sulphuric hot springs; birthplace of Enjo Zslutovodyj

Zujek — stock comic character of Pannonian folk puppet theater, a haughty townsperson representing the Zgurian stereotype; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences

Zujka — female counterpart of Zujek in Pannonian folk puppet theater; see Zguro-Deglian cultural differences
