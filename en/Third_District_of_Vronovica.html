Third District of Vronovica

The Third District (Treczi okrug) of Vronovica lays north of the river in the Zgurian part of the city.

History overview

The district's central axis is Slavjanska jezda — the former Queen Elisabeth avenue, renamed Horthy avenue under the Hungarian occupation, Lenin avenue under the Communists, Generalissimo Gowbka avenue from 1960, and restored to its present name in 1990. Running north-west as the Vronovica-Vienna route, it was in the Habsburg period a step below Franz Joseph avenue in social prestige. Its residents were professionals, minor officials, smaller merchants, and intelligentsia rather than the nobility and grand capitalists of the Second District. This made the district politically distinctive: more reformist, more pro-Slavic, and more sharply anti-Hungarian than its neighbour. The 1922 plebiscite returned approximately eighty percent pro-independence votes here.

The same milieu formed the core constituency of the Droganist movement in the 1930s. Under the Hungarian occupation, the district's monarchist population bore systematic repression — arrests, confiscations, and forced-labor assignments for those who had not departed with the government-in-exile. The survivors found the Communists the natural postwar option: the one faction credibly hostile to Hungarian restoration. The Communist government largely left the district's social composition intact; while the Second District was restocked with party loyalists, the Third retained its professional-intellectual character through four decades of socialism.

The civil war ended this continuity abruptly. In 1992 working-class Zgurian refugees from the right bank flooded the district; its Deglian residents departed under the same pressures operating everywhere across the partition line. The district is now overwhelmingly Zgurian.

Topography

Starting from Slavjanski Trug at the Ring:

The Slavic Theatre (Slavjansko Divadlo), inaugurated in 1876, was designed by the Warsaw architect Władysław Gorczyński in a Russian neo-Byzantine idiom unusual for the region. Habsburg censorship authorities suspended its operating license no fewer than four times for productions deemed politically subversive; each suspension produced demonstrations that the liberal press reported more extensively than the offensive performances themselves. The building was confiscated twice: once in the 1896 re-Magyarization campaign, and again under the Hungarian occupation of 1940–1944. Each time the theatre was shuttered and its acting company dispersed by force. The enforced silences accumulated into a theatrical history of political martyrdom whose credibility outlasted the plays that occasioned it. Since 1990 it has served as the principal cultural institution of the Zgurian Republic, producing performances exclusively in Zgurian.

The Municipal Theatre (Kostursko Divadlo), one of the city's three theatres in the early twentieth century, differed sharply from its neighbours in character. While the National Theatre performed in German and Hungarian only and the Slavic Theatre exclusively in Pannonian, the Municipal operated in all three languages and maintained a deliberately apolitical profile. It was managed in the early twentieth century by Leonard Baumgarten, father of Queen Adele. Under socialism the theatre was absorbed into the state theatrical system. Since 1995 it has operated under Zgurian Republic cultural management alongside the Slavic Theatre, and struggles for survival with residual municipal funding and shrinking audience.

Great Moravian Square (Velikomoravski Trug), the main ceremonial square of the district, opens before the Cathedral of SS. Cyril and Methodius. Its toponymic history: renamed Malinovsky Square in 1945, Tito Square in 1956, Mao Zedong Square in 1966, and restored to Great Moravian Square in 1990.

The Cathedral of SS. Cyril and Methodius, consecrated in 1932, was conceived as a national sanctuary to displace the dynastic St. Tibor's Cathedral in the fortress. The architect Isztvan Koczar combined hemispherical Byzantine domes with Art Deco ornamental motifs and Italian Rationalist structural clarity. The low hemispherical profile struck contemporary audiences as simply unfamiliar. Local opinion settled on the nickname Etelkiny cicki — "Etelka's tits," after the actress Adelj Glovacz (later Queen Adele), noted for her slight figure — which has outlasted every political change. The cathedral sustained artillery damage in 1944 and was restored in the early 1950s. Its designation as a protected cultural monument kept it open throughout the socialist period.

The Legion Square (Trug Legyi), named after the Pannonian Legion, is the site of the Legislative Assembly and other governmental buildings. Under the Communists, it was renamed Bach, Schwach and Karácsony Square (Trug Baxa, Szvaxa i Karaczoni) after three leaders of the 1919 Bolshevik uprising suppressed by the Pannonian Legion. Erwin Bach, Raphael Schwach, and Erzsebet Karácsony were Third International members who attempted to proclaim a "Pannonian Soviet Republic" but lost a short civil war to the legionaries and were shot dead the same year. Gowbka declared them heroic revolutionary martyrs and erected a monument to the trio, replacing the older legionaries' monument. In 1990 the square was restored to its original name, but the Communist monument remained.

The Legislative Assembly (Zakonodarno Zgromadjenje), completed in 1935 in stripped Rationalist style, was the First Republic's main architectural statement of sovereign ambition. Its chambers hosted the enthronement of Drogan I and the regime's political assemblies; under the Communists they accommodated the rubber-stamp parliament; since 1992 the building has served as the parliament of the Zgurian Republic. The interwar ministerial buildings at the Legion Square now house the offices of the Zgurian Republic's government: finance, interior, and foreign affairs.

The New University (Novy Unyversytet) occupies a compact quarter bounded by Slavjanska jezda, Ukraine Street (Ukrainska ulica), Russian Street (Ruska ulica), and the University Park (Unyversytecka Zagroda). It was founded in 1876 as Pannonian Pedagogical Institute by liberal pan-Slavicist professors dissatisfied with the Old University's Latin curriculum and clerical conservatism. It offered instruction in Pannonian and soon became the intellectual centre of Pannonian nationalism from the outset; the leadership of the First Republic is almost entirely drawn from its alumni. Under the First Republic it was granted university status and superseded the Old University as the primary national institution. Communists maintained this precedence on their own terms, having renamed the university after Gowbka. Since 1992 it has operated as the sole functioning university of the Zgurian Republic, its student body drawn entirely from the Zgurian zone.

The northern outskirts of the district are dominated by the Sokol Sports Complex, built around the former King Drogan Stadium (Gralicse Krola Drogana) of 1937. The Communist government retained both the stadium and the Sokol (Falcon) name, finding the pan-Slavic gymnastic symbolism convenient enough under new management. The complex is the home ground of the Zgurian Sokol football club, whose fixtures are the Zgurian Republic's most reliably attended public event.
