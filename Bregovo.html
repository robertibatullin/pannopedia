Bregovo

Bregovo (Glagolitic: ⰁⰓⰅⰃⰑⰂⰑ, from Proto-Slavic *bergъ "bank, shore") is a former town of Former Upper Pannonia.

Bregovo stood on the left bank of South Drava, 40 km east of Vronovica, opposite Gowbkaslava, originally enclosed by a near-circular river bend. After the construction of the Gowbka hydroelectric power plant raised the river level, what remained of the town drowned; only the ruins of the former bishop's castle survive, now completely surrounded by water.

History overview

Middle Ages

Inhabited from prehistoric times, the site held a Celtic settlement, a Roman castellum, and an early medieval Slavic fort with an important pagan sanctuary. Pre-Christian worship continued until 1040, when St. Tibor from Esztergom and his followers destroyed the temple and founded a Christian church; Tibor was ordained the first Bishop of Upper Pannonia, subordinate to the Archbishop of Esztergom. The new see waged a sustained struggle against the Slavic liturgy and Glagolitic tradition centered at Kadlovo monastery.

In 1115, Hungarian king Béla II granted the Bregovo bishops the title of Prince Bishop (Pannonian: knewz biskup), conferring some secular power over Upper Pannonia. They pursued authority over the lesser lords of the Pannonian territory and often maintained semi-official concubines whose children — nominally nephews or pupils — they regularly appointed as heirs. Opposing lords tended to align with Kadlovo, and their protection helped the Slavic rite survive.

The see produced a succession of notable incumbents. Miklosz (mid 12th c.) built a fortified castle, fiercely fought for sovereignty over the local lords, and personally commanded in battles. Janosz (late 12th c.) was dismissed by the Archbishop of Esztergom, refused to obey, launched an armed revolt, and won. Tamasz (mid 13th c.) was seized in Bregovo by invading Mongols but survived the siege. Benedict "the Butcher" (mid 14th c.) became notorious for harsh treatment of his subordinates. Peter "the Beardless" (early 15th c.) was widely believed to be a daughter, not a son, of the previous bishop — a legend unconfirmed, though he certainly had male partners. Djordji "the Slaver" (mid 15th c.) fabulously enriched himself by selling his subjects into Ottoman slavery, and rebuilt the Bregovo cathedral in the Florentine Renaissance style.

The late 15th century brought the most turbulent succession. Brothers Boleslav and Lukas, sons of Djordji, fought a ten-year armed conflict over the inheritance, both bribing Pope Alexander Borja. [REFINE] In 1503, Pius III dismissed and excommunicated them both and ordained an Italian bishop from outside the family, who was promptly poisoned by Boleslav's son Maczasz, who then took the seat.

Ottoman era

Maczasz "the Renegade" (early 16th c.) proved the undoing of the see: after the Ottoman victory at Mohacs, he offered the Sultan to surrender Bregovo and convert to Islam in exchange for sovereign rule over Upper Pannonia as an Ottoman vassal. He let the Turks enter the city and castle in 1529, but they pillaged and burned it, and impaled Maczasz himself by order of Sultan Suleiman. The latter despised the traitor and had no need of his service, having already established a strong hold on former Hungary.

[UNWRAP] An Ottoman military commander Haqim Abaz Bey Kuzgun, first planned to make the castle a stronghold; learned by divination that the place is cursed; founded Kuzgun Qalesi instead, future Vronovica.

From this time, the city and the castle became abandoned, the episcopal seat titular. Descendants of the late prince bishops, the Bregovy family, served Hungarian and Austrian monarchs.

Later periods

[REFINE] After the Austrian recapture of Former Upper Pannonia, some property was returned to the Bregovy family, including the ruins of Bregovo castle, but not the episcopal seat. In the mid 19th century, Lajosz Bregovy partly reconstructed the castle in neo-Gothic style, and made the family residence once again. His son Antal, a renowned traveller and collector, made it a home of his rich ethnographic collections. The castle remained a Bregovy family property until 1944. The former city of Bregovo remained an insignificant village, inhabited mostly by the castle's servants.

[EXPAND] During the 1944 German occupation, the castle was occupied by SS forces who defended it against Soviet forces for some time. The castle suffered severe damage. Collections pillaged by German and then Soviet troops. Bregovy family forced to exile by Communists, and the castle nationalized. After construction of the South Drava dam, the former town of Bregovo drowned completely, as did the lower levels of the castle.

The castle is now almost inaccessible: the only entrance lies underwater, the walls high and vertical. It is rumored to be a cursed place inhabited by ghosts; lights are sometimes reported, and voices heard. It belongs to the Zgurian state and has been on sale for 1 euro since 1998, on condition of repair within ten years. No buyer has come forward.
