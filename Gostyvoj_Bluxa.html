Gostyvoj Bluxa

Gostyvoj Bluxa (Glagolitic: ⰃⰑⰔⰕⰬⰂⰑⰋ ⰁⰎⰖⱒⰀ; born Gustav Bloch; 1830–1877) was an Upper Pannonian scholar, Slavic Revival activist, and father of Zdjeslav Bluxa. He co-founded the New University and invented bluxovica, a variant of the Pannonian Latin script now used to write Zgurian.

Gustav Bloch was born into an ethnic German craftsman's family that had settled in Vronovica in the mid-18th century. He studied at the Academy of Sciences — later the Old University — where he befriended the elder Enjo Zslutovody and his peer Dometius Bobov, becoming an enthusiastic Slavophile. He adopted a Slavic name and was an active participant in Zslutovody's "Enlightenment Society."

His relationship with Bobov lasted a lifetime, oscillating between the warmest friendship — their correspondence was at times indistinguishable from love letters — and extreme hatred, yet always collaborative and competitive.

In 1848, Bluxa enthusiastically supported the "Spring of Peoples," the Hungarian revolution, and enlisted as a volunteer in the pro-Hungarian "Zgurian Hussar" formation, named after an 18th-century Grenzer regiment. At the same time, Bobov took the opposite stance, enlisting in the volunteer "Deglian Haiduk" formation — also named after an 18th-century Grenzer regiment — in support of the Croatian forces of Ban Jelačić. Both fought at Pákozd on opposite sides. This did not prevent them from exchanging friendly letters on scholarly and philosophical topics. After the ultimate defeat of the Hungarians, Bluxa was amnestied by Franz Joseph on the strength of a petition by Lajosz Bregovy, a patron of the Slavic Revival movement.

Bluxa was a prolific contributor to The Slavic Dawn. In 1853, he published a treatise on the origins of the Zgurians and Deglians. He argued that the Zgurians descended from Scythians, speculating on a skyθ- > *zguð- > zgur- development. The name of the Deglians, he argued, was much more recent and derived from the Turkish dağlı, "mountaineer."

In Pannonian speech of the time, "mountaineer" was a synonym for barbarian. Bobov treated this as an insult to the Deglians. In the next issue, he responded with a furious article hypothesizing that the Deglians were descendants of the Dacians, and that the name of the Zgurians derived from the Slavic s gor, meaning "from mountains." Bluxa responded derisively. Bobov demanded satisfaction. They duelled with sabres, slightly wounding each other, and later expanded their arguments into full Latin-language doctoral dissertations.

In 1855, Bluxa published his Latin alphabet for the Pannonian language, arguing that Glagolitic script was difficult to write by hand and isolated Pannonians from other European peoples. This alphabet, known as bluxovica, used basic Latin characters, rendering specific Pannonian phonemes with digraphs influenced by Polish and Hungarian. In 1856, Bobov responded with his own alphabet, the bobovica, following the "one phoneme — one character" principle and employing diacritics influenced by Czech and Croatian. After some dispute, both agreed to continue publishing in Glagolitic, to avoid a split in the nascent Slavic Revival movement.

In 1860, Bluxa married Magrita Bregovska, an illegitimate daughter of his patron Lajosz Bregovy. The marriage enraged Bobov, who perceived it as a betrayal. He wrote:

For fifteen years we have quarreled, bled, and burned together in the service of truth. I do not know where I end and you begin, nor, I suspect, do you. I have known the precise light in your eyes when an argument finally yields; I have been the only witness to your mind in its darkest hours; I alone have loved you with the Platonic love that ascends where the body cannot follow. All of this — this singular, irreplaceable quintessence — you have surrendered for a pair of tits. That you find it a fair exchange, Gostyvoj, is a disappointment deeper than I possess words to name.

Bluxa treated this as an insult to his wife and demanded satisfaction. Again, they duelled with sabres, this time sustaining more serious wounds that left both unable to fence again. In 1861, Bluxa's son Zdjeslav was born, the first of six children who survived to adulthood. Bobov never married and had no children.

Despite this quarrel, both collaborated productively through the 1860s and 1870s. In 1862, Bluxa was expelled from his position at the Academy for pan-Slavic and anti-Hungarian agitation; in protest, Bobov resigned his own. Through those two decades, the Slavic Revival grew in popularity, ; both men and other members of the Enlightenment Society actively founded new associations, periodicals, and educational institutions.

In 1876, Bluxa and Bobov co-founded the Pannonian Pedagogical Institute, the future New University, funded partly by fundraising and partly by Antal Bregovy, son of Lajosz and the movement's new patron. The following year brought the final, fatal controversy.

The Russo-Ottoman Balkan War caused a sharp disagreement. Bluxa took a pro-Russian position; Bobov an anti-Russian one — not out of sympathy for the Turks, but in the hope that the Habsburgs, not the Romanovs, would be the liberators of the Balkan Slavs. In his newspaper, Bobov called Bluxa "a miserable lackey of the Muscovite Tsar"; Bluxa called Bobov "a pathetic harlot of the Turkish Sultan." Both demanded satisfaction. This time, they duelled with pistols. Bobov was killed immediately; Bluxa died of his wounds within the hour.

In 1878, both were honoured with a joint monument in front of the Pannonian Pedagogical Institute, which was named after them. After 1990, Zgurian and Deglian activists demanded the separation of the two figures, but this proved impossible without destroying the entire statue. In 1995, the monument was moved to the Neutral Zone, where it now stands in front of the Old University.
