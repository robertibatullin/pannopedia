Gostyvoj Bluxa - DRAFT

Gostyvoj Bluxa (Glagolitic: ⰃⰑⰔⰕⰬⰂⰑⰋ ⰁⰎⰖxⰀ; born Gustav Bloch; 1830–1902) was an Upper Pannonian scholar, Slavic Revival activist, and father of Zdjeslav Bluxa. He co-founded the New University and invented bluxovica, a variant of the Pannonian Latin script now used to write Zgurian.

Gustav Bloch was born into an ethnic German craftsman's family that had settled in Vronovica in the mid-18th century. He studied at the Academy of Sciences — later the Old University — where he befriended the elder Enjo Zslutovody and his peer Dometius Bobov, and became an enthusiastic Slavophile. He adopted a Slavic name and was an active participant of the "Enlightenment society."

His relationship with Bobov lasted a lifetime, oscillating between the warmest friendship — their correspondence was at times indistinguishable from love letters — and extreme hatred, with five duels to their name, but always competitive.

In 1848, Bluxa enthusiastically supported the "Spring of Peoples," the Hungarian revolution, and enlisted as a volunteer in the pro-Hungarian "Zgurian Hussar" formation, named after an 18th-century Grenzer regiment. At the same time, Bobov took the opposite stance and enlisted in the volunteer "Deglian Haiduk" formation, also named after an 18th-century Grenzer regiment, supporting the Croatian forces of Ban Jelačić. Both took part in the battle of Pákozd on opposite sides. After the ultimate defeat of the Hungarians, Bluxa was amnestied by Franz Joseph owing to a petition by Lajosz Bregovy, a patron of the Slavic Revival movement.

Bluxa was a prolific contributor to The Slavic Dawn. In 1853, he published a treatise on the origins of the Zgurians and Deglians. His hypothesis was that the Zgurians descended from Scythians, with speculation on the possibility of a skyθ- > *zguð- > zgur- development. The name of the Deglians, he argued, was much more recent and derived from the Turkish dağlı, "mountaineers."

Bobov treated this as an insult to the Deglians. In the next issue, he responded with an infuriated article hypothesizing that the Deglians were descendants of the Daci, and that the name of the Zgurians derived from the Slavic s gory, meaning "from the mountains." In Pannonian speech of the time, "mountaineer" was a synonym for barbarian.
