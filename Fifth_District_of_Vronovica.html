Fifth District of Vronovica

The Fifth District occupies the right bank of the South Drava — the Transdravian or Zadravska quarter — extending southward from the river to the northern edge of Sockostur. Its character was established in the second half of the nineteenth century by the industries that every previous administration had reason to locate away from the ordered city to the north. Brick, timber, and construction material works arrived first, exploiting the riverbank site and the rail connection; textile manufacturing, metallurgy, paper production, and an oil refinery followed through the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The workforce was drawn from the rapidly growing peasant population of the Upper Pannonian countryside. Housing followed labour wherever it settled: tenements, then barracks, built without planning supervision on whatever ground lay between the factories. By the end of the Habsburg period, Zadravska was as densely built as any quarter of the city and considerably worse serviced.

The district's labour movement was the earliest and most organized in Upper Pannonia. Trade unions rooted in the Zadravska factories formed the institutional backbone of the Social Democratic Party (SDPUP) from the 1890s; communist and anarcho-syndicalist tendencies competed within the same milieu through the interwar years. Glovacz suppressed all independent labour organizations in the mid-1930s; the Hungarian occupation maintained the suppression. Gowbka, whose political biography was inseparable from this milieu, treated Zadravska's population as a constituency that had earned his special attention.

The socialist reconstruction began in the mid-1950s. The deteriorated pre-war housing stock was progressively demolished and replaced with four-story prefabricated panel blocks — Gowbczi skrynki ("Gowbka boxes") — equipped with indoor plumbing, electricity, and gas column heating that their predecessors had often lacked. Schools, polyclinics, and workers' cultural clubs followed. The South Station was comprehensively rebuilt on its late Habsburg foundations as part of the same programme. The Gowbka boxes have since outlasted their planned service life and are in progressive disrepair.

The post-socialist transition reached Zadravska as an economic catastrophe. By 1991, unemployment in the industrial quarters ran considerably above the already severe city-wide average. Criminal organizations established control over the district's few profitable enterprises before any civil authority could contest them. The principal industrial plants, the Vronovica Oil Refinery and the Red Thread (Czrevena Nicz) Textile Factory operated under criminal protection through the civil war's worst phases without interruption, which incidentally kept ethnic violence in the district well below the levels seen to the south.

Zgurian workers, who had made up a significant share of the Zadravska labour force through the socialist period, began leaving under the pressures of the civil war. The process was complete by 1995, when the Lausanne Agreements formalized the district's inclusion in the Deglian zone. The district is now homogeneously Deglian.

The Fifth is laid out along three avenues projecting southward from the southern arc of the Bridged Ring. The easternmost one is Spiderman avenue, named by the Deglian municipal administration after one of their civil war commanders. Zseljo "Spiderman" Totov, known almost exclusively by his nickname, is a national hero in the Deglian zone and the subject of outstanding international arrest warrants everywhere else. The central one is Independence avenue (Jezda Nepodstoja), the principal axis from the Turkish bridge. The westernmost one is Leopard avenue, named after another Deglian field commander, Anton "Leopard" Pekarczik. Their previous Communist-era designations retained on Zgurian-zone maps are: Socialism avenue (Jezda Socializma) and People's Friendship avenue (Jezda Druzsby Narodov), the latter having been a scene of the bloodiest civil war atrocities. Further south, the three avenues are connected by the long arc of Worker's avenue (Robocza jezda), marking the boundary with Sockostur.

The South Station (Juzsny Kolostan), the principal terminus for the southern and eastern rail approaches to Vronovica, was built in the final years of Habsburg administration and completely rebuilt under the Gowbka government in the 1950s. The original structure was replaced with a new building in the monumental style standard for socialist-era infrastructure: a stone facade, high vaulted concourse, and decorative reliefs celebrating railway labour. It serves lines running south through the Seventh District and connecting to Deglian-controlled territory. International rail has not operated since 1992.

Beyond the station and industrial plants, the district has no significant civic or cultural landmarks. Its building stock is residential and industrial, most of it dating from the socialist reconstruction of the 1950s. A typical urban view is a panorama of endless undistinguishable blocks of dilapidated Gowbka boxes.
