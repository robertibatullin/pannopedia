Gowbkaslava

[EXPAND] Gowbkaslava (Pannonian Glagolotic: ⰃⰑⰫⰁⰍⰀⰔⰎⰀⰂⰀ, lit. "Gowbka's Glory") is an industrial city in Former Upper Pannonia. Officially renamed Novokostur (New City) in 1990 but nobody actually uses this name.

On the right bank of South Drava 40 km downstream Vronovica, in the Deglian zone. Originally, a small market town Pucza.

In 1949, CPUP approved the Party Programme for 1950-2000, including building a major industrial cluster. Pucza was logistically perfect: near the narrowing of S.Drava (convenient for building a dam), with iron ore, coal and construction materials sources nearby, and with already existing railroad. Renamed first Stalinkostur (Stalin City).

Stalin hyproelectric power plant built first [estimate plausible power output]. This resulted in rise of Drava level more than 2 meters above the dam. The Stalin Reservoir, or Pannonian Sea, created, drowining several towns including Bregovo, and turning 20 km shorelines into swamps.

Multiple factories built: steel, metallurgy, machinery, chemicals, fertilizers, plastics, fuels, later automobile plant, and classified military factories designated by numners only, like "Factory #25". These numbers were assigned unorderly and often changed in order to confuse enemy spies. In fact, they confused everybody and often caused logistical failures. Industrial waste was (in the best case) poorly filtered, making the Pannonian Sea one of the most polluted water reservoirs.

Workforce mostly recruited from countryside. Gowbka boxes massively built for workers. At first, mostly hostel-like accomodation with ca. 10 persons per room. Later, it improved with family apartments, normed 1 room apartment per family with 2 kids. In 1970, improved more with Gowbka wardrobes. [boxes and wardrobes described in Vrnovica article, TBD: separate articles].

Renamings in 1956-1957: city itself to Gowbkaslava, hydro plant to Gowpka plant, reservoir to Pannonian Sea (Gowbka didn't want to associate with it, already infamous). Drava cars [very short here, link to the main article].

1980s: constant crisis of supply of food and consumer goods. Late 1980s: open workers protests, small-scale strikes.

After Gowbka's death: plan economy collapses, most factories go bancrupt, unemployment, skyrocketing inflation, mass protests. Corrupt privatization: factories are sold by state to well-connected people for nothing. Predatory oligarchs do not invest, but disassemble and sell out. Independent worker movement emerges. Here a popular conspiracy theory: oligarchs and ruling elites fear it and try disrupt via NOON provocateurs. Play on ethnic divisions to undermine workers' common ground. It succeeds, and much more. Workers' movement disrupts in 1990 due to ethnic polarization. In 1991, violence grows like in Vronovica. In 1992 - full scale civil war. In 1993, the city captured by Deglian army, forcing Zgurians to flee.

As for now, most factories doesn't function. Stay as empty shells with all valuable equipment cannibalized. Only few metallurgical and chemical plants function, owned by undisclosured investors. As a positive side, environment became much cleaner. Hydroelectric power plant (renamed simply South Drava HEPP in 1990) still works and provides > 60% of electric energy consumed in the country, though machinery not repaired since 1985. In the recent years, cryptocurrency mining industry emerges, powered by excess HEPP output.
