Young Pannonians

Young Pannonians (Mlodopannonci) was an informal circle of Upper Pannonian Slavic Revival enthusiasts active in Vronovica in the late nineteenth century. Beginning as a student group at the newly founded Pannonian Pedagogical Institute, it soon outgrew that setting. Its prominent members were:

  • Zdjeslav Bluxa, elder son of Gostyvoj Bluxa, and informal leader of the circle until ca. 1885.

  • Kvjetana Bluxa (1862–1938), his sister.

  • Volimir Slezenj (1860–1892), novelist; author of the historical novels Burnt Alive (1884), Orsoja Drozsinj (1886), and A Slavic Litany (1888), and the unfinished coming-of-age novel The Thorny Way of an Adolescent Pannonian (written 1890–1892, three volumes published posthumously, 1895).

  • Vilmosz Komarovicki (1861–1909), composer and pianist; author of the first Pannonian-language operas, including Prince Maczasz (1884), which rehabilitates Maczasz the Renegade as a tragic figure. His other works include Zgurian Sabre Dances (1888), The Drowned World symphony (1889), and The Werewolf Hunt (1892), combining Romantic orchestration with Pannonian folk melodic material.

  • Sandor Ljux (1862–1927), playwright, director, and actor at the Slavic Theatre; author of the social dramas Peasants Are Humans Too (1885), The Uprooted Miserables (1887), A Hungarian Judgement (1889, banned for apparent anti-Hungarian sentiment), and Something Is Wrong With This World (1893), staples of the Pannonian theatrical canon.

  • Isztvan Koczar (1865–1940), painter and architect; his monumental historical canvases Manslaughter at Visztica (1890), The Fall of Bregovo (1895), and The Witch Torture in 1708 (1899) were among the most widely reproduced images of the Slavic Revival; his later architectural career culminated in the Cathedral of SS. Cyril and Methodius (1932).

  • Zlotan Imrev (1860–1936), politician; leader of the Pannonian National Union, the first legal Pannonian nationalist party, and first President of the First Republic (1920–1934).

Slezenj, Komarovicki, Ljux, and Koczar were all desperately in love with Kvjetana and dedicated works to her, establishing her name as "the primary muse of Pannonian culture." She kept all four in suspense, later justifying it as a deliberate attempt to sustain their inspiration. She married Imrev in 1884 — the least artistically gifted member of the circle, known among his peers as Pan Skuka ("Mr. Boredom"). Impressionistic paintings of nude Kvjetana by Koczar, now considered his masterpieces, were long kept in the Imrevs' private collection and never exhibited publicly until the 1950s.

The wider circle, numbering up to thirty members, organized cultural events and supported its members' creative activities. The inner circle — both Bluxa siblings, Slezenj, Komarovicki, Ljux, Koczar, and Imrev — formed a secret society built around an invented cult of the Slavic goddess Mora, with Kvjetana cast as the goddess's living embodiment.

The 1880s were the circle's productive peak, with its members creating works that became canonical in Pannonian art and literature. By the end of the decade the circle had faded: Kvjetana was increasingly occupied with home and children and with supporting Imrev's political career, while Zdjeslav and the others had moved into separate professional lives. The inner circle dissolved tragically in 1892 with Slezenj's failed attempt to shoot Kvjetana and his suicide thereafter.
