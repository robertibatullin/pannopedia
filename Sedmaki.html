Sedmaki

Sedmaki (lit. "Seveners"; masc. sg. sedmak, fem. sg. sedmajka) is a lower-class urban youth subculture of Former Upper Pannonia. Comparable in type to British chavs, Russian gopniki, and Serbian dizelashi, it is more deeply embedded in urban social life than any of those comparisons suggest, particularly in the capital Vronovica.

The subculture has both Zgurian and Deglian branches. Only Deglian practitioners use sedmak as self-description; Zgurian members reject the term as an insult associated with the Deglian south and designate themselves instead as kto xodi po rextu — "those who go by rules" — or, more compactly, rextovy, "rule-abiding." Despite the naming dispute, both branches share an identical code of conduct.

Origins

The subculture emerged in the Seventh District of Vronovica — West Sockostur — in the 1980s. The eight-story Gowbka wardrobe blocks (Gowbczi szatniki) of the Sockostur housing programme had been allocated to construction, production and transport workers, recruited mostly from countryside. This milieu produced uprooted cynical youth, asserting themselves through demonstrative toughness and delinquent behavior.

The subculture skyrocketed in 1990, immediately following the collapse of the Communist regime, and fully manifested itself in massive street violence of that period. By 1992, it faded away as the Zguro-Deglian conflict evolved from pogroms and block-to-block fights into full-scale warfare of organized armies. Unstructured street groups had no place in this new scale of violence. Some sedmaks turned professional criminals, others paramilitaries; the line between those roles was rarely clear. Surviving Zgurian sedmaks followed other Zgurians north as the Deglian advance consolidated the southern zone, and their arrival visibly changed the social atmosphere of the Third and Fourth Districts. Through the 1990s and into the early 2000s, the spremniks dominated the streets — the militarized nationalist subculture whose wartime organization suited the emergency. Once the conflict stabilized and the emergency receded, the sedmaki revived and have prevailed since.

Appearance

The male sedmak appearance is standardized: head shaved or closely cropped; body muscular and heavily tattooed; clothing consisting of fake-brand sportswear in the community's colors — red and white on the Zgurian side, blue and white on the Deglian; gold neck chains uniformly fake. The female sedmajka appearance is equally recognizable: tight and revealing clothing, heavy cosmetics, flashy jewelry, stereotypically dyed blonde hair with visible roots. Compliance is enforced through peer pressure and deviations are noted.

Tattoos carry communal affiliations, territorial claims, and standing within the hierarchy. They also serve as the primary eligibility marker for Neutral Zone exclusion: UN police apply a standing ban on visibly tattooed individuals, which in practice prevents Deglian and Zgurian sedmaks from encountering one another, since the Neutral Zone is the only transit route between the two zones.

Lifestyle

The sedmak lifestyle stands in deliberate contrast to the spremnik ideal. Where spremniks are ascetic, sedmaks drink, smoke, and stay out late. Where spremniks maintain collective military discipline, sedmaks observe the rext code — which governs behavior without resembling a command structure.

Music occupies a central role. The genre known as FUPcore is the dominant musical form of the subculture — heavily amplified, combining Pannonian folk melodic fragments with electronic production, shouted vocals, and bass output calibrated for automotive playback. It is performed at volumes that share the experience with surrounding streets on an involuntary basis.

The standard sedmak car is a used Lada or Niva — valued for its price, Slavic origin, and structural tolerance for modification — rebuilt from the interior outward: a sound system of grossly disproportionate power, bodywork repainted in community colors, aftermarket suspension, external lighting designed for announcement rather than visibility. Ownership of an adequately outfitted car is a primary status marker, and for a local mini-group leader, a social necessity.

Community life is conducted in public: at fixed outdoor meeting points, specific street corners, and the pavement in front of food kiosks. Evenings extend to no fixed endpoint. Social standing is maintained through continuous physical presence.

Women are not full members of the sedmak community. Access runs through male partners: a woman may enter a group as the girlfriend of a male member; there is no other route. Within the community she is treated as the property of her partner, with no voice in collective affairs and no standing in disputes. In some of the worst districts, sedmak affiliation through a partner is the only available source of physical protection for young women, and it is sought on those terms.

The rext code

The rext (from German Recht, "right") is the organizing code of the sedmaks: a binary classification of all persons, objects, relationships, and behaviors into two fundamental categories.

Everything is either vajs (from German weiß, "white") — clean, permissible, admirable — or szvarc (from German schwarz, "black") — contaminating, forbidden, defiling. Contact with something szvarc alters one's standing in the community. The reflexive verb for this — sja oszvarcuje — implies defilement as an internal transformation, not an external judgment.

The primary vajs–szvarc axis follows the Zguro-Deglian color taboo in its most extreme form. For Deglian sedmaks, everything Zgurian is szvarc; for Zgurian rextovy, everything Deglian. This does not mean conflict is avoided: violence against the enemy community is vajs, or at minimum neutral. What defiles is the wrong kind of contact — admiration, consumption, affiliation — not confrontation.

The szvarc sphere extends beyond the ethnic division. Everything associated with non-European races is szvarc, and this may represent the code's oldest semantic layer, predating the football-club color conflict.

A distinct but overlapping category is szvulj (from German slang Schwule, "gay"). The szvulj category covers homosexuality and everything associated with LGBTQ+ identity, and extends to all behaviors, manners, and appearances perceived as soft, cultivated, unmanly, nonviolent, law-abiding, tolerant, or intellectual. Reading for pleasure is szvulj. Declining a fight is szvulj. Wearing a suit is szvulj. Preferring wine to rakia is szvulj. The category is not applied satirically. It reflects a coherent worldview in which masculinity is defined by specific markers and anything outside that cluster is its negation.

Szvulj items and behaviors are not necessarily szvarc — defiling through contact — but are despised, rejected, and subjected to sustained harassment.

Violations of rext are handled according to their nature. Inadvertent or isolated lapses are punished by beating and humiliation, but atonement and pardon are possible through recognized rituals. Deliberate and public violation — contempt for rext rather than failure to comply — is treated as blasphemy. Lethal responses are standard.

Brand prohibitions

The rext extension of the Zguro-Deglian color taboo is applied more strictly and further into consumer culture than in the general population. Everything associated with the enemy's colors is szvarc; everything associated with cultural aspiration or stylishness is szvulj. Both disqualify without appeal.

Sportswear brands of German or American origin are vajs in their standard colorways; items in the enemy's colors are szvarc regardless of brand origin. Asian manufacturers are szvarc without exception. French and Italian manufacturers are szvulj regardless of colorway.

Technology brands are classified by color association. Facebook and Telegram are szvarc for Zgurian rextovy on blue-and-white grounds; Deglian sedmaks ban Coca-Cola, Nintendo, and Colgate on red-and-white grounds. The color-inversion utilities used in the general population circulate through both communities for the same reasons.

Cars are evaluated by national origin: German and American manufacturers are vajs; Asian manufacturers are szvarc; French and Italian manufacturers are szvulj. Russian manufacturers are borderline: eastern enough to create classificatory difficulty, but affordable enough for rext to relax, which accounts for the prevalence of Russian vehicles in the subculture.

Relations with spremniks

In practice, the primary enemy of a sedmak is not a sedmak of the other community — the UN tattoo policy prevents this encounter — but a spremnik of his own. Spremniks intervene against sedmak behavior in areas where both subcultures are present, without warning and physically. Sedmaks return the attention.

Spremniks are szvulj by nearly every rext criterion: they tolerate homosexuality, respect women, abstain from alcohol and tobacco, follow organizational hierarchies, and care about ideology. That such an apparently szvulj person is fully capable of hospitalizing a sedmak is a complication the rext framework does not resolve tidily.

Street encounters between the two groups are common and reliably violent. The spremniks have discipline and sobriety; the sedmaks have numbers.

Relations with civilians

Sedmaks are generally feared and hated by most of the civilian population. They are simply an outright physical threat. Nevertheless, exactly this reason makes them attractive for lower-class youth. Moreover, in the worst parts of the Seventh and the Fifth Districts affiliation with a sedmak community is less a lifestyle choice than a survival calculation. A young man who does not affiliate becomes the primary target of the harassment that affiliation would have deflected.

After approximately twenty-five, sedmaks usually normalize or turn professional criminals. Normalization means employment, family, reduced street activity — but not a change of values. Former sedmaks continue to observe the rext distinctions in consumer choices, in their attitudes toward the other community, in what they regard as szvulj and in what they find acceptable in the behavior of people around them. They fail to report violence they witness as something ordinary. They discourage in their children any interest that would read as szvulj: reading beyond functional necessity, academic ambition, non-violent conflict resolution, mixed-community socialization. They vote, where they vote at all, for candidates who do not threaten these values. The subculture reproduces itself not primarily through active recruitment but through the households of its veterans.
