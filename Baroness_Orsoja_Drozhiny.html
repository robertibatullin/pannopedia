Baroness Orsoja Drozsiny

Baroness Orsoja Drozsiny (Zgurian; Deglian: Orsoja Drožiny; Glagolitic: ⰑⰓⰔⰑⰡ ⰄⰓⰑⰆⰊⰐⰬ; Hungarian: Drozsinyi Orsolya; 1499-1536), born Orsola di Buonasperanza, was an Upper Pannonian noblewoman of the early 16th century. Her biography is little known and heavily mythologized.

Historical evidence

Orsola di Buonasperanza was born in a Dalmato-Italian patrician corsair family in Rijeka. In 1516, she was given in marriage to Upper Pannonian Baron Miklosz Drozsiny, owner of the castle Drozsin in Nexozsi Vrjexi mountains. In 1526, Baron Drozsiny was killed in the battle of Mohács. Ottomans seized most of the Hungarian Kingdom, including Upper Pannonia. Orsoja inherited the castle as the single heir of her husband; other male relatives were killed or had fled to the Royal Hungary, and there were no children.

The Ottomans did not approach Drozsin castle situated in the mountain forest wilderness. In the following years, according to a few surviving Austro-Croatian and Ottoman documents, the castle became a robber's nest. The robbers, dressed in wolf skins and calling themselves vlokodlaci (werewolf), attacked Christians and Muslims on both sides of the frontier.

After Kuzgun Qalesi was founded in 1534, Drozsin acquired strategic value, lying near the mountain pass on the road from Kuzgun Qalesi towards the Adriatic Coast. The robbery could no longer be tolerated. In 1535, Abaz Bey Kuzgun besieged the castle. Orsoja did not surrender. The siege lasted six months until all defenders died from starvation.

In legends

In local folklore, Orsoja is depicted as "Werewolf Widow" and "Cannibal Baroness", said to have been an actual werewolf who forced her captives to eat human flesh, thus turning them also into werewolves. She is said to have been the last survivor of the siege, having cannibalized all other castle residents. The ruins of the castle itself, destroyed by the Turks, were long considered cursed.

In literature

In 1886 Volimir Slezenj, a writer and Slavic Revival enthusiast of the Bluxa circle, published the historical novel "Orsoja Drozsiny" in which he depicted her as a heroic figure of national anti-Turkish resistance. Her "werewolf" robbers were presented as patriotic partisans, and the whole legendary tradition was dismissed as Turkish propaganda. This book was influential; "Dawn over the Drava", the first Pannonian sound film starring Etelka Baumgarten, was its adaptation.
